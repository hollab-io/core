# Spec 05 — Governance Process

> Maps to **Holacracy Constitution v5.0, Article 5**

---

## Overview

Changing a Circle's Governance requires the **Governance Process**. This is the most critical process in Holacracy — it governs how Roles, Policies, and structure evolve. It operates through consent-based decision-making (Integrative Decision-Making), not majority vote.

---

## 5.1 Governance Participants

### Circle Members

A Circle's **Circle Members** are:

-   Partners filling the Circle Lead Role
-   Each Partner serving as Role Lead for a Role in the Circle
-   If a Role has multiple Role Leads, a Policy MAY limit how many represent that Role

### 5.1.1 Circle Reps

-   Any Circle Member MAY request an election for a **Circle Rep** at any time.
-   The Circle Rep represents the Sub-Circle in its Super-Circle.
-   Purpose: "Tensions relevant to process in a broader Circle channeled out and resolved"

**Accountabilities:**

1. Seeking to understand Tensions conveyed by Role Leads within the Circle
2. Discerning Tensions appropriate to process in the broader Circle
3. Processing Tensions within the broader Circle to remove constraints on the Circle

**Election Rules:**

-   MUST use the Integrative Election Process (unless Policy defines alternate).
-   Only Circle Members are eligible.
-   Circle Lead MAY NOT serve as Circle Rep.
-   Max one Circle Rep per Circle (unless a Policy of a containing Circle allows more).
-   The elected Circle Rep becomes a Circle Member of the containing Circle.
-   A containing Circle MAY limit Circle Reps from becoming members (only if Roles have comparable representation).
-   Circle MAY add Accountabilities/Domains to Circle Rep Role but NOT change Purpose or remove constitutionally-defined Accountabilities.

### 5.1.2 Facilitator & Secretary

-   Facilitator: accountable for facilitating the Governance Process.
-   Secretary: accountable for capturing/publishing Governance outputs; holds Domain over governance records.
-   Any Circle Member MAY request an election at any time.
-   MUST use Integrative Election Process.
-   No Role or Policy MAY assign these via other means.
-   Normally only Circle Members are eligible candidates (Policy of Circle or Super-Circle MAY expand/limit).

---

## 5.2 Scope of Governance

Circle Members MAY, through the Governance Process:

1. Define, amend, or remove the Circle's **Roles**
2. Define, amend, or remove the Circle's **Policies**
3. Move Roles or Policies **into** a Sub-Circle (only if they enact that Sub-Circle's Purpose/Accountabilities)
4. Move Roles or Policies **out of** a Sub-Circle (only if no longer relevant to that Sub-Circle's Purpose/Accountabilities)
5. Hold **elections** for elected Roles within the Circle

**No other decisions** are valid Governance outputs.

### 5.2.1 Scope of Policy

A Policy MAY only be:

1. A constraint on authority of one or more contained Roles; or
2. A grant of Circle/Circle Lead authority to one or more Roles; or
3. A grant of authority to control/impact Circle Domains (or constraint on how); or
4. A rule changing a default Constitution rule/process (only where explicitly allowed).

**Scope rules for Policies:**

-   A Policy granting/constraining authority applies recursively to all Sub-Circles (unless stated otherwise).
-   A Policy changing a default Constitution rule applies only within the holding Circle (unless explicitly stated to apply recursively).
-   Sub-Circles MAY override a recursively-applied Policy via their own Policy (unless the original explicitly forbids it).

## 5.3 Changing Governance

### Asynchronous Proposal Flow

1. A **Proposer** (Circle Member) shares a **Proposal** in writing with all other Circle Members.
2. Communication channel MUST be allowed by the Secretary.
3. Other Circle Members may:
    - Ask clarifying questions
    - Share reactions
    - Raise concerns about adopting the Proposal
4. Each concern is an **Objection** if it meets the criteria (§5.3.2).
5. If **no Objections** from any Circle Member → Proposal is **adopted** and amends Governance.
6. If **Objections are raised** → Proposer and each Objector must find a way to address them.
7. After integration effort, all Circle Members get another chance to raise Objections.
8. A Circle MAY adopt a Policy defining a **time limit** for raising Objections (no response after deadline = no Objection).
9. Any Circle Member MAY request the Proposer bring the Proposal to a **meeting** for real-time processing.
10. Unless Policy says otherwise, async processing stops and Proposal is withdrawn until re-proposed at meeting.

**Representation rule:** Circle Members may only make Proposals or raise Objections representing Roles they fill as Role Lead or as Circle Rep. They MAY also temporarily represent a Role with permission from one of its Role Leads.

### 5.3.1 Proposal Requirements

A valid Proposal requires the Proposer to:

1. Describe a **Tension** the Proposal would address for one of the Proposer's Roles
2. Share an **example** of an actual past or present situation illustrating the Tension
3. Give a **reasonable explanation** of how the Proposal would have reduced the Tension in that example

If the Facilitator determines a Proposal doesn't meet these criteria → discard it.

### 5.3.2 Objection Requirements

A concern counts as an Objection only if the Objector provides a reasonable argument for ALL of:

1. The Proposal would **reduce the capacity** of the Circle to enact its Purpose or Accountabilities
2. The Proposal would **limit the Objector's capacity** to enact the Purpose or Accountability of a Role they represent (even if they filled no other Roles)
3. The concern **does not already exist** without the Proposal — it would be _created_ by adopting it
4. The Proposal would **necessarily cause the impact**, or if it _might_, the Circle wouldn't have adequate opportunity to adapt before significant harm

**Exception:** A concern ALWAYS counts as an Objection if adopting the Proposal would violate a rule in the Constitution.

### 5.3.3 Testing Potential Objections

-   The Facilitator MAY test whether a concern meets Objection criteria.
-   The Facilitator judges only whether logical arguments were presented for each criterion.
-   The Facilitator MAY NOT judge accuracy or importance of arguments.
-   For constitutional-violation Objections: Facilitator MAY ask the Secretary to interpret; if Secretary rules no violation → Facilitator MUST dismiss.

### 5.3.4 Rules of Integration

While resolving an Objection:

1. Facilitator MUST test an Objection if requested by any Circle Member; discard if invalid.
2. Objector MUST attempt to find an amendment resolving both the Objection and the Proposer's Tension. If not making good faith effort → Facilitator deems Objection abandoned and drops it.
3. Any Circle Member MAY ask clarifying questions about the Tension or examples. If Proposer not answering in good faith → Facilitator deems Proposal dropped.
4. Objector MAY suggest an amended Proposal with reasoning. Proposer must then present a reasonable argument for why it would fail. If unable/unwilling → Facilitator deems Proposal dropped.

### 5.3.5 Integrative Election Process

For electing Circle Rep, Facilitator, or Secretary:

| Step | Name                         | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| a    | **Describe Role**            | Facilitator identifies Role and term. No comments on candidates.                                                                                                                                                                     |
| b    | **Nominate Candidates**      | Each Circle Member nominates one candidate via ballot (labeled with own name). No abstaining or multiple nominations.                                                                                                                |
| c    | **Nomination Sharing Round** | Each nominator states why their nominee is a good fit. No commenting on others' nominees. No responses.                                                                                                                              |
| d    | **Nomination Change Round**  | Any Circle Member MAY change their nomination with reason. No responses.                                                                                                                                                             |
| e    | **Make a Proposal**          | Facilitator counts nominations, proposes candidate with most. Tie-breaking: (i) self-nominated → propose them; (ii) current holder → propose them; (iii) random selection; (iv) go back and ask tied-candidate nominators to change. |
| f    | **Objection Round**          | Facilitator asks each Circle Member for Objections. If Objections: allow discussion or discard. If discarded, go back to (e) ignoring that candidate.                                                                                |

**Policy options:**

-   A Circle MAY define a time limit for nominating/responding (non-respondents excluded).
-   Facilitator is accountable for triggering new elections when terms expire.

### 5.3.6 Surrogates for Facilitator & Secretary

When a surrogate is needed (Role unfilled, unavailable, or requests it), precedence order:

1. Someone specified by the person to be replaced
2. For Facilitator → acting Secretary; for Secretary → acting Facilitator
3. Circle Lead (or first Circle Lead to volunteer if multiple)
4. First Circle Member to declare themselves surrogate

## 5.4 Governance Meetings

### Purpose

Real-time enactment of the Governance Process. Complements async processing.

### Scheduling

-   Secretary is accountable for scheduling regular Governance Meetings.
-   Secretary MUST schedule special Governance Meetings promptly upon Circle Member request.
-   Requester MAY specify intention and limits (e.g., focus on specific Tension or specific Roles).
-   Special meeting authority is constrained to stated intent/limits.

### 5.4.1 Attendance

-   All Circle Members may participate.
-   Acting Facilitator and Secretary may participate even if not Circle Members (temporary membership for duration).
-   Circle Rep MAY invite one Partner from an immediately-containing Circle to join, if:
    -   Processing a specific Tension affecting the Circle Rep's Circle
    -   Circle Rep senses this Tension and believes it makes sense to process
    -   Invited guest represents the Circle Rep's Circle (temporary Circle Member for duration or until invitation withdrawn)
    -   Guest may only participate while processing that specific Tension
-   No one else may participate.

### 5.4.2 Notice & Duration

-   Meeting requires reasonable advance notice to all Circle Members.
-   No quorum required (unless Policy specifies one).
-   Meeting ends at scheduled duration; Secretary MAY extend only if no Circle Member objects.
-   Absent Circle Members count as having had opportunity to raise concerns.

### 5.4.3 Meeting Process

| Step | Name                             | Description                                               |
| ---- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| a    | **Check-in Round**               | Each participant shares current state. No responses.      |
| b    | **Agenda Building & Processing** | Build agenda of Tensions, then process each in turn.      |
| c    | **Closing Round**                | Each participant shares closing reflection. No responses. |

-   **Time Out**: Any participant may request; Facilitator may grant/deny. May discuss admin issues or Constitution rules. May NOT work on Tensions/Proposals/Objections. Facilitator may end at any time.
-   A Policy MAY add to this process but NOT conflict with Article 5 rules.

### 5.4.4 Agenda Building

-   Facilitator solicits agenda items from all participants **in the meeting** (not beforehand).
-   Each item = short label, no explanation or discussion.
-   More items may be added between processing.
-   Regular meeting → Facilitator chooses order (but election requests go first on request).
-   Special meeting on request → requesting participant chooses order.
-   Items processed one at a time.
-   Elections → use Integrative Election Process.
-   Everything else → use Integrative Decision-Making Process.

### 5.4.5 Integrative Decision-Making Process (IDM)

| Step | Name                     | Rules                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ---- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| a    | **Present Proposal**     | Proposer describes Tension and presents Proposal. May allow others to help craft it. Focus only on Proposer's Tension — no other Tensions or concerns.                                                                                                                                                      |
| b    | **Clarifying Questions** | Others ask questions to understand. Proposer may answer or decline. No reactions/opinions. Participants may ask Secretary to read Proposal or show Governance.                                                                                                                                              |
| c    | **Reaction Round**       | Each participant (except Proposer) shares reactions, one at a time. No dialog, no reactions to reactions.                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| d    | **Option to Clarify**    | Proposer may share comments and amend Proposal. Amendments must primarily address Proposer's Tension (not others'). Only Proposer and Secretary may speak.                                                                                                                                                  |
| e    | **Objection Round**      | Each participant raises concerns one at a time. Facilitator captures as Objections or tests them. No discussion beyond Objector during testing.                                                                                                                                                             |
| f    | **Integration**          | Focus on each Objection one at a time. Brainstorm amendments. Objection resolved when Objector confirms amended Proposal wouldn't trigger it AND Proposer confirms it still addresses the Tension. Apply rules of integration (§5.3.4). After all resolved → back to Objection round with amended Proposal. |

## 5.5 Process Breakdown

A **Process Breakdown** occurs when a Circle shows a pattern of behavior or output violating Constitution rules.

### Who Can Declare

-   Facilitator or Secretary of the Circle (for own Circle or any Sub-Circle)
-   Any concerned Partner MAY request a Facilitator to review a Sub-Circle
-   Facilitator is accountable for auditing Sub-Circle meetings/records on request

### 5.5.1 Breakdown from Failed Governance

-   Facilitator MAY declare Process Breakdown if a Proposal fails to reach resolution after reasonable time and effort.

### 5.5.2 Process Restoration

When Process Breakdown is declared, the following authorities activate:

1. Facilitator gains authority to judge logic/relevance of arguments for Proposals and Objections in the Circle.
2. Super-Circle Facilitator gains a Project to restore due-process.
3. Super-Circle Facilitator gains authority to take over as Facilitator or Secretary of the Circle.
4. Super-Circle Facilitator MAY assign an additional Circle Lead for the duration (their decisions supersede other Circle Lead decisions).

These authorities END when due process is restored (assessed by Super-Circle Facilitator).

If the Circle has no Super-Circle → powers vest in its own Facilitator.

### 5.5.3 Escalation of Process Breakdown

A Process Breakdown in a Circle is NOT automatically a breakdown in the Super-Circle. However, if it remains unresolved for an unreasonable time → Super-Circle is ALSO considered in Process Breakdown.

### System Requirements

-   The system MUST implement the full Proposal lifecycle: creation → objection period → integration → adoption/rejection.
-   The system MUST enforce Proposal requirements (Tension + example + explanation).
-   The system MUST enforce Objection requirements (4 criteria + constitutional violation exception).
-   The system MUST implement the Integrative Election Process.
-   The system MUST implement Governance Meetings with the IDM process.
-   The system MUST support Process Breakdown declaration and restoration.
-   The system MUST track all Governance changes as an immutable audit trail.

## State Machine: Proposal Lifecycle

```
Draft → Active → [No Objections] → Adopted
                → [Objections Raised] → Integrating → [Resolved] → Active (re-check)
                                                     → [Unresolvable] → Discarded
                → [Pulled to Meeting] → Withdrawn (re-propose at meeting)
                → [Invalid] → Discarded
```

## State Machine: Election Lifecycle

```
Nominating → Sharing → Changing → Proposing → ObjectionRound → [No Objections] → Complete
                                                              → [Objection, resolve] → Complete
                                                              → [Objection, discard candidate] → Proposing (next candidate)
```

## On-chain Commitments Surface

The data structures and events above describe the full Holacracy IDM model.
The current contract set implements a **strict subset** of that surface — the
subset that represents _commitments_ rather than _coordination_.

### Design stance: commitments on-chain, coordination off-chain

Holacracy's IDM is fundamentally a meeting protocol. Present → Clarifying
Questions → Reaction Round → Option to Clarify → Objection Round →
Integration. Every step is a human speaking to other humans. None of it
belongs on a blockchain. What does belong on-chain is the audit trail of
what was committed to: _a proposal was created, it reached these objections,
they were resolved in this order, it was adopted (or discarded) at time T_.

The contract implements this subset. Everything else — the rounds, the
clarifying questions, the integration discussion, the process-breakdown
escalation, the election nominations — lives in the meeting room, captured
as off-chain records pointed at by content hashes when persistence is needed.

### What the contract emits

`packages/contracts/src/contracts/MeetingFactory.sol` exposes five proposal
lifecycle events and five corresponding entry points:

| Step            | IDM mapping (§5.4.5) | Contract event                                                                                                | Entry point             |
| --------------- | -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| a               | Present Proposal     | `ProposalCreated(proposalId, orgId, circleId, proposer, proposerRoleId, tensionHash, changeType, changeData)` | `createProposal(...)`   |
| e               | Objection Round      | `ObjectionRaised(objectionId, proposalId, objector, concernHash)`                                             | `raiseObjection(...)`   |
| f               | Integration          | `ObjectionResolved(objectionId, proposalId, resolvedBy)`                                                      | `resolveObjection(...)` |
| § 5.2           | Enactment            | `ProposalAdopted(proposalId, orgId, resultId, adoptedBy)`                                                     | `adoptProposal(...)`    |
| § 5.3.1 / § 5.5 | Discard              | `ProposalDiscarded(proposalId, orgId, discardedBy)`                                                           | `discardProposal(...)`  |

Steps b, c, and d — Clarifying Questions, Reaction Round, Option to Clarify
— produce no on-chain artifacts because nothing committed changes. If a
specific facilitator's notes need to be preserved they can be referenced by
a content hash in a later amendment, but the baseline spec does not require
it.

### Content-address discipline

`tensionHash` (on `ProposalCreated`) and `concernHash` (on `ObjectionRaised`)
are `bytes32` content-addresses of the off-chain text. The contract is
indifferent to the addressing scheme — it works equally with:

-   `keccak256(utf8(rawText))` when the content is inline and small
-   CIDv1 sha256-truncated to 32 bytes when stored on IPFS / Filecoin
-   0G Merkle root when stored on 0G Storage

This keeps `ContentRef` polymorphic across storage backends. See
`CLAUDE.md` and `hollab-sdk` for the encryption layer that sits between the
plaintext and the storage backend.

### Authorization

| Action             | Who can call                                                        |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `createProposal`   | Any org member                                                      |
| `raiseObjection`   | Any org member                                                      |
| `resolveObjection` | Original objector (withdrawal) OR org admin (integration confirmed) |
| `adoptProposal`    | Org admin only                                                      |
| `discardProposal`  | Org admin only                                                      |

The `resolvedBy` address in `ObjectionResolved` lets indexers and UIs
distinguish a withdrawal from an integration without a separate event or
status enum entry.

### Non-enforcement: "no adopt with unresolved objections"

The spec says a proposal with valid outstanding objections must not be
adopted. The contract does **not enforce this**. Reasons:

1. The constitution's definition of a "valid" objection is judgment-based
   (§5.3.4 criteria) — the contract cannot reliably determine validity
   without duplicating the meeting facilitator's role on-chain.
2. On-chain enforcement would require iterating objections in `adoptProposal`
   (an SLOAD loop) for a rule that the coordinator is already applying.
3. The event log preserves full auditability: anyone can read the proposal
   and objection trail and determine whether the adoption was procedurally
   valid.

The contract _does_ enforce cheap state-machine invariants:
`Draft → {Adopted, Discarded}` (no double-transition, no discard-after-adopt);
`Raised → Resolved` (no double-resolve); objections can only be raised on
Draft proposals. These are one-SLOAD checks and catch the common bugs.

### Out of scope (for now)

The following belong to the full IDM spec above but are **not** implemented
on-chain in the current contract set. Each is either a coordination concern
(off-chain by design) or a post-MVP expansion:

-   Process Breakdown declaration / restoration / escalation
-   Integrative Election Process — the contract has a generic `Election`
    `ChangeType` that records an outcome, but the nomination / reaction / final
    round mechanics happen in the meeting room
-   Objection validity tests (the 4 criteria in §5.3.4)
-   `Withdrawn` proposal status — the MVP uses `Discarded` for both
    admin-initiated rejection and proposer withdrawal, distinguishing them via
    the `discardedBy` address in the event

### Only entry point

`createProposal` + `adoptProposal` (or `discardProposal`) is the **only**
path to structural change. There is no "execute directly" shortcut: any call
that mutates the `RoleRegistry` must first leave a `ProposalRecord` on-chain
with its tension hash, change payload, and proposer. Callers that want a
one-signer "draft and adopt in the same user action" flow batch the two
writes — the seed script, the authed meeting room, and the SDK example all
do this — but the two records are always distinct at the contract layer.
